CHAPTER 01

Introduction to GoHighLevel

What HighLevel is, why it was built, its founders and background, its strengths and weaknesses, its market position, and where it's headed.

SECTION 1

What Is GoHighLevel?

GoHighLevel — which the company itself now more often presents simply as HighLevel — is an all-in-one business, sales, marketing, CRM and automation platform.

In simple terms: GoHighLevel is software built to bring together a large number of separate business tools into one system.

Normally, a business uses a different tool for every job:

CRM software Email marketing tool SMS tool Website builder Funnel builder Appointment booking tool Zapier-style automation Review management software Client communication inbox

HighLevel's basic idea is:

CORE IDEA "Bring these many disconnected tools into one ecosystem."

Officially, HighLevel brings together CRM, pipelines, workflows, website/funnel builder, conversations, AI, forms, calendars, payments, reputation management and other business tools inside one platform.

SECTION 2

What Purpose Was It Built For?

Its original purpose was specifically to solve a problem faced by digital marketing agencies.

Imagine an agency with 20 clients. For every single client, the agency has to manage:

Leads Facebook leads Website leads Emails SMS Follow-ups Appointment bookings Sales pipeline Client communication Landing pages Reviews Automations

If every one of these lives in a different piece of software, the agency's work becomes complicated. HighLevel was built to solve exactly this problem — giving agencies a centralized platform to manage their clients' sales and marketing systems.

According to the company's own history, the founders saw that agencies were relying on a patchwork of tools for CRM, automation, messaging and client management. HighLevel was developed to simplify that complexity.

SECTION 3

When Was GoHighLevel Built?

HighLevel was founded in 2018. Since then:

  1. 2019 Public launch of the platform — the company reported 1,000+ customers.
  2. 2020 The platform kept growing through the pandemic, reportedly supporting 5,000+ customers.
  3. 2021 Growth driven by SaaS Mode and advanced agency-focused features.
  4. 2022 The company reported crossing 20,000+ customers.
  5. 2023 A reported milestone of 60,000+ customers, driven by community-led growth.
  6. 2024–2025 Major expansion of AI features. Per the company, by 2025 the platform was powering 2.2 million businesses and had processed billions of leads.

Important note: these numbers are the company's own reported milestones — they shouldn't be taken as independently audited market figures.

Understanding this difference is very important.

What Is an Agency Account?

Think of the Agency Account as the main control center.

EXAMPLE You start a digital marketing agency — Samavia Digital Solutions — and create a GoHighLevel agency account. You now have multiple clients: Ali Real Estate, ABC Dental Clinic, FitLife Gym, and XYZ Coaching. You manage all of them through your Agency Account.
Samavia Digital Solutions Agency Account
↓
Ali Real Estate
ABC Dental Clinic
FitLife Gym
XYZ Coaching

Samavia Digital Solutions is the Agency Account — the individual businesses underneath it are Sub-Accounts.

What Is a Sub-Account?

A Sub-Account is basically a separate workspace for one individual client.

EXAMPLE Your client is ABC Dental Clinic. You'll create a separate HighLevel Sub-Account for them, containing their own Contacts, CRM, Pipelines, Workflows, Website, Funnels, Forms, Calendars, Emails, SMS, Conversations, and Payments — in short, that client's complete business system.

Agency Account vs. Sub-Account

AGENCY ACCOUNT For the agency owner · Manages multiple clients · Handles overall administration · Can create sub-accounts · Controls agency-level settings
SUB-ACCOUNT For an individual business/client · Manages one specific business · Handles day-to-day client operations · Usually doesn't manage other sub-accounts · Controls client-level settings
SIMPLE FORMULA Agency Account = the owner of the building. Sub-Account = one office inside that building.

PART 4

What Is a CRM?

CRM stands for Customer Relationship Management. In simple terms: a CRM is a system where a business manages all the data about its customers and leads.

For each customer, a business typically holds information such as:

Name Email Phone number Business name Interest Purchase history Messages Appointments Notes Tags Sales status

If all of this lives scattered across Excel, WhatsApp, email, notes, and Facebook, managing the business becomes difficult. A CRM organizes all of it in one place.

What Does GoHighLevel's CRM Actually Manage?

CONTACTSWho is the customer?
OPPORTUNITIESHow big is the potential sale?
PIPELINESWhat stage is the sale at?
CONVERSATIONSWhat communication has happened with the customer?
TASKSWhat does the team need to do?
NOTESInternal information related to the customer.
TAGSCategorizing contacts.
PART 5

What Is a Contact?

This is a very important concept.

DEFINITION Contact = a record of a person or business

Example: Name — Ali Khan · Email — ali@gmail.com · Phone — 0300XXXXXXX · Interest — Website Development. This person is a Contact in the HighLevel CRM.

Where Can a Contact Come From?

A contact can be added manually, or automatically:

WEBSITE FORMSomeone fills out a form → a contact is created automatically.
FACEBOOK LEADSomeone fills out a Facebook lead form → a contact is created in the CRM.
APPOINTMENT BOOKINGSomeone books an appointment → a contact is created.
IMPORTYou import contacts from an Excel/CSV file → they land in the CRM.

What Can Show Up in a Contact's Profile?

Inside a contact you can see:

Name Email Phone Address Tags Notes Appointments Conversations Emails SMS Opportunities Payments Activities Custom Fields

In other words, HighLevel can organize a customer's entire journey into one central record.

PART 10

How Pipelines and Workflows Connect

This is one of HighLevel's most powerful combinations. Suppose an opportunity's stage changes:

STAGE: NEW LEAD The workflow can automatically send a welcome email, notify the salesperson, and add a tag.
STAGE: MEETING BOOKED The workflow can send a confirmation email, a calendar reminder, and an internal notification.
STAGE: WON The workflow can send a welcome email, an onboarding form, run client-onboarding automation, generate an invoice, and notify the team.

In short: the pipeline shows the business process, and the workflow automates that process.

SECTION 1

What Is Automation?

Automation simply means: a system that performs a task automatically when a certain event happens, without a human having to do it manually every time.

EXAMPLE A customer fills out a form on a website. Normally an employee would have to manually check the form, add the customer's data to the CRM, send an email, inform the salesperson, and follow up.

But with automation:

Contact Created
↓
Welcome Email
↓
SMS Sent
↓

PART 13

The Difference Between API and Webhook

This is a very common source of confusion.

API You ask the system for information. Example: an external app asks, "Give me Ali's information," and HighLevel responds with Name, Email, Phone.
WEBHOOK The system automatically sends information on its own. Example: New Contact Created → data is sent automatically.
SIMPLE RULE API = you ask for data. Webhook = the system automatically sends data after an event.

PART 17

Common Automation Mistakes

Mistake 1 — Duplicate Workflows

Unnecessarily letting the same contact enter multiple workflows. Result: Email, Email, SMS, SMS — the customer gets confused.

Mistake 2 — No Exit Condition

The customer has already purchased, but the system is still asking "Are you interested?" — it doesn't look professional.

Mistake 3 — Wrong Trigger

You wanted the workflow to start on "Form Submitted," but accidentally used "Contact Created" as the trigger — now the workflow can run for unexpected contacts too.

Mistake 4 — No Testing

Testing a workflow directly on a live customer can be risky.

Mistake 5 — Too Much Automation

Not everything should be automated — for example, sensitive client complaints may need human intervention. The best system is:

Automation
+
Human Control

PART 5

What Is SaaS?

SaaS stands for Software as a Service. In simple terms: the customer doesn't buy the software and install it permanently on their own computer — instead, they access it through a subscription.

EXAMPLE A customer pays a monthly subscription of $97/month and gets access to the software.

SaaS Mode in the Context of GoHighLevel

Here's the interesting part. Suppose you launch your own software brand — Samavia Business CRM — while the underlying technology is built on the HighLevel ecosystem. You sell it to your clients as:

Samavia Business CRM$97 / month
↓

↓

↓
Customer gets CRM access

What Does the User See?

From the client's perspective, they simply see Samavia Business CRM. They don't necessarily need to see the underlying technology the same way the agency owner does.

This can help an agency build a product-based, recurring revenue model.
